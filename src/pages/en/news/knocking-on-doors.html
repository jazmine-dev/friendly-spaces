Knocking on doors

September 2026 · Jazmine, founder

By August 2025 the idea was well developed and I had run out of reasons not to test it. I gave myself a goal: eight to ten places, enough to prove the concept. Then I started knocking on doors in Bern. The first few stayed shut. The owners were polite, they were busy, and a family-friendly label from a person they had never heard of was not on their list. Then I knocked on the right one, the Orangerie Elfenau.

Emily Wisson had just taken over as manager there earlier in the year, and she answered the way you hope someone will answer when you are pitching an idea for the first time. I was nervous. I had believed in the project for months in private, and this was the first time I had to believe in it out loud, across a table, to someone who could say no. By the end of the meeting she had said yes twice: yes to the pilot, and yes to trying the first Friendly Spaces Playbox. I walked out thrilled, and with better questions than I had walked in with. Her feedback was the push I needed to look beyond Bern, where I found a group of like-minded people who understood what I was trying to do before I had finished explaining it. I thank every one of them for taking it seriously.

Basel, four at once

By the end of 2025 the list had grown by four: Klara, the Markthalle Basel, Love is Sweet Atelier and the Magical Cafe. Each of them already had a space for families. What they had in common was someone who cared about it.

At Klara the play area is looked after by the manager and updated continually. I was impressed by how clean it was, and then I learned why: the play mats are professionally cleaned on a regular basis. Downstairs, the Klara Studio has lawn bowling and pinball for older children, so the fun does not stop the day a child outgrows the play mats.

The Markthalle has its play area next to the Finkmüller Cafe, a miniature Markthalle for its smallest guests. The bigger ones take the slide from the main floor down to the lower level. The team was responsive from the first conversation, and this year they built a new changing area right next to the play corner. No Friendly Space made more improvements for families in the past twelve months. Congratulations, Markthalle.

Love is Sweet was our first creative atelier. Derya Sahin, the owner, runs birthday parties for children and a space where parents can come and be creative with their babies. This year she offered her first workshop for mothers and babies, and over the past twelve months she has held regular workshop days with childcare included. There is a quiet corner for nursing and there are high chairs, so even the tiniest guests are provided for.

The Magical Cafe was a gem to find: one of the first play cafés in Basel, with a very large indoor play area, birthday parties and delicious baked goods. What impressed us most was their commitment to the neighbourhood. They hold events for Halloween, Christmas and Easter and flea markets through the year, usually with some entertainment and a photo session so families can take the occasion home with them.

Each place was so individual that together they made a rich group of pilot businesses. Since January we were lucky to welcome three more: Crafty Crew Toddler Workshops, Sempre Berna and the Bridge in Zurich.

Learning from the biggest one

The Bridge was our first custom play area. We set up three larger play items along with a Friendly Spaces Playbox that gets checked more often than usual. It also taught us the most. In a venue with that much traffic, everything has to be robust enough to survive a great many small hands, and our first check surprised us. We had to sit down and work out how to look after venues of that size. It was one of the most valuable experiences of the pilot.

The latest trial was a mobile play corner, built for #hashtagviedesparents' first aid event for parents. It worked. There were many little visitors, and their parents were happy to discover a playful corner at such an event. I had met Cleo Cuadra, the owner of #hashtagviedesparents, near the end of 2025, and she has been a great partner since, supporting the idea and spreading the word in her network. We have not yet made it to the Romandie, but we have leads, and it is the next stop on the way to connecting families with the places that welcome them.

The pilot ended this summer. We learned more than we expected, and we are motivated to move into the next phase.

Jazmine founded Friendly Spaces in Bern. If you run a place that could be Friendly, write to her.
