Knocking on doors

August 2026 · Jazmine, founder

By August 2025 the idea was well developed and I had run out of reasons not to test Friendly Spaces in the real world. I gave myself a goal: eight to ten places, enough to prove the concept. Then I started knocking on doors in Bern. The first few stayed shut. The owners were polite but busy, and a family-friendly label from someone they had never heard of was not on their list. And then I knocked on the right one: the Orangerie Elfenau.

The manager had taken over only a few months earlier, and she answered the way you hope someone will when you pitch an idea for the first time. By the end of the meeting she had said yes twice: yes to the pilot, and yes to trying the first Friendly Spaces Playbox. I walked out thrilled, with plenty of useful feedback and better questions than I had walked in with. Her input gave me the push I needed to look beyond Bern. Further afield, I found like-minded people who understood what I was trying to do before I had finished explaining it. I am grateful to every one of them for taking the idea seriously.

Basel, four at once

By the end of 2025 the list had grown by four: Klara, the Markthalle Basel, Love is Sweet Atelier and the Magical Cafe. Each of them already had a space for families. What they had in common was someone who cared about it.

At Klara the manager looks after the play area personally. I was impressed by how clean it was, and then I learned why: the play mats are professionally cleaned on a regular basis and the toys are refreshed all the time. Downstairs, the Klara Studio has lawn bowling and a second pinball machine for older children, so the fun does not stop the day a child outgrows the play mats.

Next to the Finkmüller Cafe, the Markthalle has a play area built as a miniature Markthalle for its smallest guests. Bigger children can take the slide from the main floor down to the lower level. The team was responsive from the first conversation, and this year they built a new changing area right next to the play corner. No Friendly Space made more improvements for families in the past twelve months. Congratulations, Markthalle.

Love is Sweet was our first creative atelier. Its owner hosts children’s birthday parties and runs a space where parents can come and be creative with their babies. This year she held her first workshop for mothers and babies, and she now runs regular workshop days with childcare included. There is a quiet corner for nursing as well as a high chair, so even the tiniest guests are looked after.

The Magical Cafe was a gem to find. It is one of the first play cafés in Basel, with a very large indoor play area, and the team hosts birthday parties and makes delicious baked goods. What impressed me most was their commitment to the community. They hold events for Halloween, Christmas and Easter, as well as flea markets throughout the year, usually with some entertainment and a photo session so families can take a memory home with them.

Each place was so different that together they made a wonderfully varied group of pilot partners. Since January we have been lucky to welcome three more: Crafty Crew Toddler Workshops, Sempre Berna and the Bridge in Zurich.

Learning from the biggest one

The Bridge was our first custom play area. We set up three larger play pieces, along with a Friendly Spaces Playbox that we check more often than usual. It has also taught us the most. In a venue with that much traffic, everything has to be robust enough to survive a great many small hands, and our first check was a surprise: a lot of pieces had broken or gone missing. We had to sit down and work out how to look after venues of that size. It was one of the most valuable lessons of the pilot, and we are now working on the next version for venues like this one.

Our most recent trial was a mobile play corner, built for a first aid event for parents run by #hashtagviedeparents. It was a great success: there were lots of little visitors, and their parents were happy to find a play corner for even the smallest of them. I met Cleo, the owner of #hashtagviedeparents, near the end of 2025, and she has been a great partner ever since, supporting the idea and spreading the word through her network. We have not yet made it to French-speaking Switzerland, but we have leads, and it is the next stop on the way to connecting families with the places that welcome them.

This summer we wrapped up the pilot. We learned more than we expected, and we are excited about what comes next for Friendly Spaces.

Jazmine founded Friendly Spaces in 2025. If you run a place that could be Friendly, write to her.
