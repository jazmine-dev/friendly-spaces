About

Public spaces, with kids in mind

Friendly Spaces began with a question every parent asks on a Saturday morning: where can we go where the kids are actually welcome — not just tolerated?

The story

It started in a café queue

Cities are full of beautiful cafés, shops and cultural spaces — and full of families quietly leaving them because there was nowhere to park the stroller, nothing for a three-year-old to do, and a look from the counter that said please don't. We didn't think that was a problem of bad places. We thought it was a problem of no standard, and no way for good places to be found.

So we built both: a label with clear criteria, earned on site, and an app that shows families exactly where the label is. We started in Bern and Basel with a pilot group of partners who cared about the same thing we did. The pilot is over now; the label is open to every space that wants to be Friendly.

What we believe

Three things we don't compromise on

Every pin is earned

No place gets on the map by paying for it. The label is scored on site, against the same 48 points for everyone — and re-checked.

Small changes, real difference

Most of what makes a place family-friendly costs little: a high chair, a corner, a team that smiles. We help partners find those changes first.

Families are the city

Public space that works for a stroller and a toddler works better for everyone. We want Swiss cities to be places where kids are visible, not hidden.

Who's behind it

Hi, I'm Jazmine

I founded Friendly Spaces in Bern, out of equal parts frustration and stubbornness. My background is in design and digital work, which is why the label came with an app from day one: a standard nobody can find is just an opinion.

Today the Friendly Spaces team visits every partner personally — we're the ones with the clipboard, the tape measure and the toddler's-eye view. If you run a space, we'd love to see it. If you're a parent, tell us where we should look next.
